How to use Read Aloud

This tool reads your writing out loud so you can catch mistakes with your ears. When you hear your own words, missing words, doubled words, and clunky sentences jump out in a way they never do on the screen. Everything runs from this drive — no internet, no account, no installation, and nothing gets saved onto the computer you plug it into unless you ask.

1. Starting it

Plug in the drive, open it, and double-click the launcher for your computer:

ComputerOpen this
WindowsSTART - WINDOWS.exe
MacSTART - MACOS.app
LinuxSTART - LINUX.sh

Your web browser will open with the Read Aloud page. The first start takes a few seconds while the voice loads from the drive — the status card in the top corner tells you when it's ready.

If Windows shows a blue warning ("Windows protected your PC"), that's SmartScreen being cautious about an app it hasn't seen before. Click More info, then Run anyway. On a Mac, if it says the app can't be opened, right-click the app and choose Open once — after that it starts normally.

Don't open the files inside the shared folder directly — always use the START launcher.

2. Your first read

  1. Paste your writing into the big text box (Ctrl+V, or ⌘V on a Mac).
  2. Click where you want the reading to begin.
  3. Press F8 — or click the ▶ Read button.

The voice reads from your cursor to the end, and the sentence being spoken is highlighted so your eyes can follow along. Press Esc to stop. When you stop, a small ▸ marker stays on that sentence so you can find your place again — press F8 to carry on from there.

On many laptops the F-keys need the Fn key held down (so Fn+F8). If a key seems to do nothing, that's usually why. The on-screen buttons always work. Press ? in the app to see all the shortcuts.

3. The controls

The dark bar under the text box has the everyday controls:

ControlWhat it does
VoiceWhich voice reads to you. More voices can be added to the drive later.
SpeakerAppears only for voices that contain several people — pick whichever you like. Switching is instant.
DeliveryAppears for some voices. Natural is lively; Steady is flatter and more even — many people find Steady better for proofreading, because the words stand out more than the performance.
SpeedHow fast it reads. Slower catches more; faster is good for a final pass.

Click the small Playback and Display headings to open more: Volume and Pitch, the Pause between sentences, Step mode (it waits after each sentence until you press F8 or Space — good for careful passes), Focus mode (dims everything except the sentence being spoken), text size, spacing, font, and the light/dark theme.

4. Marking things to fix

The whole point is fixing your writing, so you can mark trouble without stopping the reading:

  • Hear a clunky sentence? Press F9 — it gets flagged and the voice keeps going.
  • When you're done listening, press F10 to jump to the next flagged sentence, or click Show to open the list of everything you flagged and click any entry to jump there.
  • Flags stick to their sentences even while you edit — rewrite the sentence and the flag follows it.
  • Report saves all your flagged sentences as a text file on the drive.
  • F7 replays the current sentence if you want to hear it again.

The app also quietly checks for doubled words ("the the"), double spaces, and repeated punctuation, and underlines them in the text. The Tidy text button straightens curly quotes and removes invisible characters that sometimes sneak in when pasting from Word or the web.

5. Making an audio file

Want your document as audio — for listening on a walk, or for someone else?

  1. Click ⟳ Render / Update. Every sentence is recorded into memory (a progress count shows).
  2. Click Export WAV or Export MP3.

The finished file is written to a saves folder on this drive, next to the app — the status line tells you the exact spot. If you edit the text afterwards, Render / Update only re-records the sentences you changed (they glow briefly so you can see it), which makes fixing one word in a long document fast.

6. Saving your work

  • Save session to drive (in Settings, pronunciation & sessions) saves your text, your flags, and your settings together as one file on the drive. Open session brings it all back — on any computer.
  • Autosave is off unless you turn it on. Switched on, it quietly saves your session to the drive every minute (a green dot shows while it's active), and next launch offers to restore it.
  • Copy all text puts your whole draft on the clipboard; Save draft as .txt writes it to the drive as a plain text file.
  • You can also drag a .txt or .md file from your computer straight onto the text box to open it.
Nothing is saved anywhere by itself. If you close the page with unsaved work, the app warns you first — but your text lives only in that browser tab until you save it.

8. Your privacy, plainly

Your text never leaves the computer: no internet is used, nothing is uploaded, and there is no account. The app writes files only to this drive, and only when you press a save or export button (or turn autosave on). It does not write to the computer you're using. One honest note: the computer itself is outside the app's control — for example, Windows clipboard history (Win+V) can remember text you copy or paste anywhere.

9. If something isn't working

  • The page says "Start with the launcher" — you opened a file inside the shared folder directly. Close it and double-click the START launcher instead.
  • No sound — check the Volume slider in Playback, and the computer's own volume.
  • F-keys do nothing — hold Fn with them, or use the on-screen buttons.
  • "The drive could not be written" — the drive was unplugged or is write-protected; the file went to the computer's Downloads folder instead, and the status line says so.
  • The voice never finishes loading — eject the drive safely, plug it back in, and start again. If it keeps happening, re-copy the app onto the drive.

10. Adding more voices

Voices live in the voices folder on the drive — each one is a folder you copy in, and it appears in the Voice menu on the next launch. Some voices contain many speakers in one package (the Speaker menu appears automatically). The included voice package maker page can build a package from downloaded voice files and let you listen before installing; there's a link to it in the app's "Keyboard and troubleshooting" section.

Read Aloud is free to use and runs entirely from this drive. Happy proofreading.
